Grocery prices in Australia

Same trolley, different bill

By Jin XIE · October 2026

Australian food prices rose 3.0% in the year to August 2026. Hardly anyone's shopping went up by exactly that much, though. What you pay depends on what goes in the trolley, which chain you shop at, and whether your suburb has a supermarket at all.

Most of the price data comes from the Consumer Price Index (CPI), which the ABS uses to track how the cost of a fixed set of household goods changes over time.

Only three foods got cheaper. Eggs fell furthest, down 4.7%. Pork and other cereal products slipped just below zero.

Ten foods barely moved, rising by less than 2%. Fruit, vegetables, seafood and coffee all sat in this band.

Eight foods rose 2–5%, close to the overall food rate of 3.0%. Restaurant meals (4.3%) and soft drinks and juices (4.0%) led this group.

Four foods rose 5% or more. Beef and veal and lamb and goat both jumped 9.4%, and milk rose 7.7%.

The same month held both extremes. A 14.1-point gap separated eggs from beef and lamb.

BACK TO THE TROLLEY

So, did your shopping go up 3%?

Probably not by exactly that. Beef and lamb rose 9.4% over the year to August 2026, while eggs got cheaper. A trolley heavy on beef and lamb went up by more than the average. One that is mostly eggs, bread and fruit went up by less.

Where you shop matters more. A year of 3% inflation adds about $2 to a $75 basket. Going to the cheapest chain instead of the dearest saved $17 to $34 on the same 17 items in June 2026.

That only helps if there is a supermarket nearby. About 557,000 people in Greater Melbourne live in an area without one, from new estates in Wollert and Tarneit North to older suburbs like Brighton East.
