Grocery prices and supermarket access

Food prices are still rising, but not evenly. This story follows the Consumer Price Index (CPI), the ABS measure of how the price of a fixed basket of household goods changes over time, from the national food bill down to which Melbourne suburbs have a supermarket nearby.

Only three foods got cheaper. Eggs fell furthest, down 4.7%. Pork and other cereal products slipped just below zero.

Ten foods barely moved, rising by less than 2%. Fruit, vegetables, seafood and coffee all sat in this band.

Eight foods rose 2–5%, close to the overall food rate of 3.0%. Restaurant meals (4.3%) and soft drinks and juices (4.0%) led this group.

Four foods rose 5% or more. Beef and veal and lamb and goat both jumped 9.4%, and milk rose 7.7%.

The same month held both extremes. A 14.1-point gap separated eggs from beef and lamb.

WHAT THIS MEANS

Three things to take away

The national food inflation rate is a useful headline, but what a household pays depends on what it buys, where it shops and where it lives.

01

The headline hides the spread

Food prices rose 3.0% over the year to August 2026, but egg prices fell 4.7% while beef and lamb rose 9.4%. The average sits between foods that moved in opposite directions.

02

Where you shop matters more than a year of inflation

At 3.0%, a year of food inflation adds roughly $2 to a $75 basket. In June 2026, the dearest chain charged $17 to $34 more than the cheapest for the same 17 items, depending on the state.

03

Access depends on the suburb

About 557,000 Greater Melbourne residents live in an SA2 with no supermarket. They range from growth areas such as Wollert and Tarneit North to established suburbs such as Brighton East.
